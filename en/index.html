A forgotten product range

Technical leather in everyday factory work

Leather served many purposes in factories: it sealed moving machine parts, connected components, protected surfaces and was made into parts exposed to particularly heavy wear.

The Gebrüder Krafft price list of 1913 brings this variety to life. This exhibition presents selected products using contemporary catalogue pages, distinguishing information found in the catalogue from additional technical explanations.

The product range

Six examples of technical leather goods

Leather cups & seals

The price list includes leather cups for pump pistons, hydraulic presses and piston seals. Their shapes and dimensions varied according to their intended use.

Leather cords & thongs

Drawn and twisted leather cords, together with other leather articles, formed part of the range. The catalogue lists different designs and diameters.

Leather fittings & equipment

The illustrated plates document a wide variety of cut and shaped leather parts for industrial and commercial use.

Belt accessories

Belt tensioners, fasteners, adhesive and tools were used to fit, join and maintain leather belts.

Other factory supplies

The catalogue also lists leather rings, discs, aprons and waterproof boots, illustrating the breadth of the company’s industrial and commercial range.

Loom pickers

Pickers for mechanical looms formed a separate line of production. Their particular history is documented on a dedicated website.

Original source

The 1913 price list

The publication bears the title Preis-Liste für technische Leder und Lederwaren and was issued by the Gebrüder Krafft leather works in Fahrnau, Baden.

It records not only product names but also dimensions, variations, prices and illustrations. These details make it a valuable source for the history of industrial leatherworking.

Historical context: A price list documents the products offered in its year of publication. By itself, it does not establish production volumes or identify the factories that actually purchased particular items.
Original: Gebrüder Krafft leather and leather-goods factory, 1913 price list (scan from the research collection).

Context

One material, many industries

Krafft’s technical leather goods show how closely tanning, mechanical engineering and factory operations were connected.

This page focuses on the documented product range and its technical functions. The history of loom pickers is covered separately, as is the development of leather transmission belts.

Sources and methodology

Documentation, not reconstruction

Principal source: Gebrüder Krafft, leather works, Fahrnau (Baden): Preis-Liste für technische Leder und Lederwaren, 1913 edition. Catalogue pages used: 18–24 and the accompanying illustrated plates.

Additional source: Gebrüder Krafft: 75th-anniversary publication, 1854–1929. Among other topics, it describes the development of various tanning and manufacturing activities.

Historical product names are reproduced as faithfully as possible. General explanations of their functions provide technical context, not evidence of specific deliveries to individual businesses.

Note on the illustrations: All historical product illustrations shown here come from the 1913 Krafft catalogue. None is a modern reconstruction.
